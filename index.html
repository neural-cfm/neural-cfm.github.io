Synthesizing Speech from Intracranial Recordings with Conditional Flow Matching

Neural speech decoders are usually trained to regress audio features from brain activity, which averages over everything the recording cannot pin down and yields blurry, muffled speech. We instead train a conditional flow-matching model that transports Gaussian noise to a speech spectrogram, guided by the neural data; the usual regression decoder turns out to be the special case of a flow trained only at its first step. Below are reconstructions from stereo-EEG in two speaking participants and from intracortical Utah arrays in a participant with dysarthria.

Pipeline

In every example, rows share one colour scale; words in red are Whisper transcription errors against the reference, and WER is per utterance. All spectrograms and audio are pitch-shifted for de-identification.

Utah arrays: dysarthric participant

Public dataset of Wairagkar et al. (2025): four Utah arrays in ventral precentral gyrus. Held-out day 172; our decoder is strictly causal. The target is text-to-speech of the cue aligned to the attempted speech, so the reference transcript is the cue itself.

Stereo-EEG: speaking participants

Each trial is one sentence read aloud and then repeated from memory. To protect the participants, only pitch-shifted spectrograms are shown, without audio. With no ground-truth transcripts, the reference is Whisper's decode of the vocoded ground-truth spectrogram. Trials are the best, median and worst by our per-utterance WER among held-out trials with at least five distinct reference words (leave-one-block-out cross-validation).
